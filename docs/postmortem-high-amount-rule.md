# Postmortem: Legitimate Purchases Blocked by Rules Release 2026.10.2

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Status | Resolved |
| Severity | SEV2 (partial impact on a core service, many customers affected) |
| Impact window | 2026-10-14 15:00 UTC until hotfix `2026.10.3` (released 2026-10-15) |
| Services | `POST /risk/decisions` |
| Evidence | `examples/false-positive-investigation.md`, `examples/hotfix-verification.md` |

This is a lab scenario. The figures come from the sample traffic in `examples/decision_events.log`, generated by `scripts/simulate_traffic.py`. Details the lab doesn't record, such as who reported what and when, are written as they would play out in a support team.

## Summary

Rules release `2026.10.2` was meant to raise the weight of the `HIGH_AMOUNT` signal from 20 to 45 points. The same change also set the signal's limit to 100 instead of 1000. From then on, any purchase over $100 scored as high value. Ordinary checkouts went to manual review or were declined, and the approval rate fell from 95.8% to 37.5%.

Hotfix `2026.10.3` restored the 1000 limit and kept the intended weight increase. Replaying the incident traffic on the hotfix approves every decision that was wrongly blocked.

## Impact

- **Approval rate:** 95.8% before the release and 37.5% after it, a drop of 58.3 points. Reviews rose 50.8 points and declines rose 7.5 points.
- **Wrongly blocked decisions:** 145 decisions would have been approved on the previous rules: 129 sent to review and 16 declined. They came from 128 customers, and the median purchase was $196.19.
- **Customer experience:** a review is still a blocked checkout for the end user. Most of the impact was delayed or abandoned purchases, not outright declines, so a decline-rate alert alone would have understated the problem.

## Root Cause

The release combined an intended change (raising the weight) with an unintended one (the limit). Nothing checked how the new rules would score real traffic before they went live, so a change that blocked most ordinary purchases shipped without warning.

Contributing factors:

- **No pre-release replay.** Replaying recent traffic against the new rules would have shown the approval rate collapsing before any customer was affected.
- **The limit and the weight changed in the same edit.** Reviewers were looking at the weight change, and the limit change was easy to miss in the diff.
- **No test pinned the limit.** A test asserting that an ordinary $150 purchase is approved would have failed on `2026.10.2`.

## What Went Well

- Every decision logged its reason codes, rules version, and full scoring input. That made it possible to explain each decision and replay all of them on another rules version.
- Metrics broken down by rules version showed the cause within one query.
- The investigation produced exact impact numbers and a list of affected events for customer follow-up, instead of an estimate.

## What Could Be Better

- The approval-rate alert originally waited 10 minutes. It now pages after 5, because blocked checkouts cost customers revenue every minute.
- Customers learned about the problem from their own users. A status page update should go out as soon as the alert confirms platform-wide impact.
